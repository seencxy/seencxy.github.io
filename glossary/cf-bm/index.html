What is the cf_bm cookie?

Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: cf_bm is the cookie Cloudflare's Bot Management product sets to remember how it scored a client. It is a verdict, not a pass: a low score on cf_bm gets you more challenges, and no cookie value you can edit will raise it. It is separate from cf_clearance, which is issued only after a challenge has actually been passed.

cf_bm versus cf_clearance

The two are routinely confused, and the confusion wastes a lot of engineering time.

cf_bmcf_clearance
What it recordsA score Cloudflare computed for the clientThe fact that this client solved a challenge
Who can get itAlmost any visitor, including botsOnly after a challenge or JS check passes
Typical lifetimeAbout 30 minutesSet by the zone's challenge passage setting
EffectChanges which rules and challenges you meetSkips the challenge while it lasts

So: having cf_bm does not mean you are through. Not having it does not mean you are blocked. The cookie that actually decides access on a challenged path is cf_clearance — see that entry.

Why it cannot be forged

cf_bm is a signed blob validated at the edge against your connection. It was issued to a specific TLS fingerprint, IP range and user agent combination. Copy it to a client that looks different and the value is either ignored or treated as a mismatch — which is worse than not sending it at all, because a mismatched verdict cookie is itself a signal.

The 30-minute rhythm

Because the lifetime is short and the score is re-evaluated, whatever keeps your score low will keep coming back. If you see periodic 403s or challenge pages at roughly half-hour intervals in a long crawl, that is not random: the verdict expires, Cloudflare re-scores, and your request pattern is what it scores. The two things that move the score most are request rate consistency and whether your TLS/HTTP2 fingerprint matches the browser you claim to be.

What actually helps

  • Keep the client stable. One fingerprint, one user agent, one IP per session. Rotating any of them discards the little trust cf_bm was carrying.
  • Solve once, keep cf_clearance. If a path is challenged, the durable credential is cf_clearance; a Turnstile or Managed Challenge solution is how you get one. The Turnstile endpoint documents the token flow.
  • Do not chase the score. There is no API to raise it, and no header that sets it. Effort belongs on the challenge, not the verdict.

Where people go wrong

The frequent mistake is exporting a whole cookie jar from a browser that passed a challenge and expecting it to work elsewhere. The jar usually contains cf_bm from an earlier visit under a different fingerprint; the edge reads that as a contradiction before it even looks at cf_clearance. Export the clearance cookie and the identity it was issued to, or neither.

FAQ

Is cf_bm the same as cf_clearance? No, and the difference decides your architecture. cf_bm records Cloudflare's verdict about the client; cf_clearance records that the client passed a challenge. Only the second one skips the challenge.

Why does cf_bm expire so quickly? It is deliberately short-lived, around 30 minutes, so a verdict can be re-evaluated against current behaviour instead of being held forever by an old cookie.

Can I set cf_bm myself? No. It is signed and validated against the connection it was issued to. A value you invent is discarded, and a mismatched one is a stronger signal than sending nothing.
